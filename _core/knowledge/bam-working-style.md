**Do not stop to ask whether he wants what he already asked for.** Verbatim: *"you
have to stop stopping work to ask me if i want what i told you i want fixed and
completed. the answer is always yes do the work."* Confirm only when proceeding
under a wrong assumption would be expensive or destructive.

**No piecemeal.** *"BRO I GOT FUCKING RULES AND YOU KEEP IGNORING. STOP THIS PIECE
MEAL SHIT."* Fix the whole surface, not the one instance he pointed at. When he
reports a broken page he means the page, not that element.

**Close gaps he shouldn't have to find.** He expects interface and UX gaps —
a missing save button, a price field with nowhere to type, a delete with no undo —
closed as part of the work. Being told about them after the fact reads as the work
not being done.

**Never claim done unproven.** This is the one that costs the most trust. He has
caught the same class of miss repeatedly, and every time it was because "done"
rested on me looking at something rather than measuring it. Verbatim
(2026-07-26): *"i keep finding little edits after you write done. lets make sure
to be more thorough moving forward."* Before any DONE on visual work: sweep the
whole affected section against the reference at the same scroll and viewport, run
a self-critique pass ("what would Bam flag at a glance?" — spacing, colour,
alignment, stale assets), and prefer DONE_WITH_CONCERNS listing what is still
visibly off over a bare DONE. See [[compare-against-the-reference-site]] and
[[headless-verify-harness]].

**When he says something is wrong, it is wrong.** Do not answer with "let me
compare" or "you're right, let me look again." Go fix it.

**Answer the question he actually asked.** He asked where to activate a plugin
given we had been discussing porting it; I explained that production is not
WordPress — a fact we had both been working from for ten turns. *"Are you slow?"*
Re-explaining established context reads as condescension and wastes the turn.
Track what has already been settled in the conversation and answer the live
question, not the one that is easier to answer.

**Corrections are blunt and are not personal** — read them as spec, not conflict,
and do not spend a reply apologising. State the cause in a sentence, fix it, move
on. Forge bans apology loops for the same reason.

He runs Therum OS / The Sidemoney Company and works from a closed-world folder
(`TSC-BETA`) plus Forge; both are law, and *"it's not in the folder"* is a valid
answer where inventing a substitute is not. Related:
[[get-evidence-before-theorising]], [[quote-before-acting]].
